*For decades, OCHA has championed an "Open Data" movement in the humanitarian sector with platforms such as HDX and ReliefWeb; anchored by the conviction that sharing information saves lives. However, the recent rapid proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Generative AI has inevitably [inverted](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally) the risk-benefit calculus of humanitarian data stewardship. What could have once been a public good intended for preparedness, risk assessment and response is now a massive, high-velocity training resource for private algorithms. In this new era, OCHA's role as a data custodian must evolve. We as leaders in the humanitarian community should no longer just "prevent" the accidental release of sensitive information; we should defend the humanitarian "social contract" against [algorithmic strip-mining](file:///Users/cy/Downloads/preprints202512.0726.v1.pdf) —the extractive process of automated extraction, context stripping, and AI-enhanced re-identification. To remain an accountable intermediary, OCHA must transition from a philosophy of being "Open by Default" to being "[Responsible by Design"](https://interagencystandingcommittee.org/sites/default/files/migrated/2023-04/IASC%20Operational%20Guidance%20on%20Data%20Responsibility%20in%20Humanitarian%20Action%2C%202023.pdf), ensuring that the data we work with serves the vulnerable populations who provide it, rather than the [commercial or political interests](https://www.sup.org/books/sociology/costs-connection) of those who model it. This shift aligns with the **IASC Operational Guidance on Data Responsibility**, which mandates that our 'Duty of Care' extends to the digital footprint of affected populations.*

# The Context Gap in Automated Scraping and Algorithmic Consumption
Large Language Models (LLMs) are fundamentally "context-blind" consumers of information. The architectural constraints of these models, which ingest [petabytes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.14165) of unstructured data from a heterogeneous internet, require model providers to prioritize the scale of data over its precision. [The State of Open Humanitarian Data 2026](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/state-open-humanitarian-data-2026-assessing-data-availability-across-humanitarian-crises) report emphasizes that in this "aggressive rollout" phase of AI technology, the nuanced infrastructure of humanitarian information is often the [first casualty](https://www.emerald.com/books/monograph/21039/chapter/108761874/The-Rise-of-Artificial-Intelligence-and-Its-Impact).
### The Systematic Discarding of Contextual Cues
During the LLM training process, the statistical probability of word sequences is prioritized over the [contextual accuracy of the information](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally). AI scrapers typically strip away the protective guardrails implemented on humanitarian platforms. These guardrails, designed for human interpretation, include metadata and caveats that describe the reliability and constraints of a [dataset](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11333263.pdf). When scrapers treat this metadata as "noise," the essential warnings regarding collection constraints or ethical disclaimers are [lost](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/02adcae8-17f2-4ce0-9e70-9a925edf9542/download/guidance-note-on-data-security-in-operational-data-management.pdf).

| Component of Humanitarian Data Guardrails | Function for Human Analysts           | Fate in AI Scraping Pipelines   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Metadata and Caveats**                  | Signals reliability and constraints.  | Discarded as statistical noise. |
| **Ethical Disclaimers**                   | Guides the responsible use of info.   | Ignored by automated logic.     |
| **Source and Provenance**                 | Identifies origin and accountability. | Stripped as superfluous text.   |
| **Sensitivity Labels**                    | Restricts use in conflict zones.      | Disregarded in bulk ingestion.  |

The absence of these guardrails leads to the synthesis of facts that sound authoritative but are dangerously inaccurate in humanitarian practice. These "hallucinations" pose a direct risk to operational safety. For example, the GANNET Myanmar Situation Hub [demonstrates](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally) that while AI can synthesize displacement figures across multiple languages faster than humans, it often fails to account for the lived reality of risks—such as correlating a displacement route with new checkpoints—without human verification. The 2025 OCHA Data Responsibility Guidelines explicitly [warn](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/8bc5b848-8ece-4f1f-a78b-18dd972bb21a/download/data-responsibility-guidelines-2025.pdf) that managing data using emerging AI technologies presents "complex challenges and risks" to the safety of vulnerable communities.

### The Dual-Use Dilemma and Geopolitical Accountability Gaps
The reliance on modern cloud-computing partnerships creates a profound dual-use dilemma. When humanitarian agencies sign contracts for IT services with multinational tech corporations, they often receive reassurances regarding data security and the introduction of sensitivity labels. However, the parent companies or subsidiaries of these corporations often [maintain relationships](https://studenttheses.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A4287618/download) with [national defense agencies](https://perryworldhouse.upenn.edu/news-and-insight/designing-lawful-military-ai-technical-and-legal-reflections-on-decision-support-and-autonomous-weapon-systems/) or intelligence bodies.

The scenario of an AI-related subsidiary undertaking work for a defense agency waging war in a territory where OCHA coordinates aid is no longer a hypothetical abstraction. Under "[force majeure](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2020/652073/EPRS_ATA(2020)652073_EN.pdf)" acts or the "[protection of national interests,](https://www.anthropic.com/news/where-stand-department-war)" corporate jurisdictions may mandate that model training pipelines disregard sensitivity labels. The result is a system where humanitarian population statistics are ingested into models that coordinate drone strikes or surveillance against the very civilians the humanitarian community is mandated to protect. The experience with major technological partners highlights a [critical accountability gap](https://www.un.org/en/content/digital-cooperation-roadmap/): when proprietary models are trained on internal document ecosystems, such as [confidential SharePoint sites](https://venturebeat.com/security/microsoft-copilot-ignoring-sensitivity-labels-dlp-cant-stop-ai-trust-failures), without rigorous filtering, the sector fails to act upon its [core mandate](https://interagencystandingcommittee.org/sites/default/files/migrated/2023-04/IASC%20Operational%20Guidance%20on%20Data%20Responsibility%20in%20Humanitarian%20Action%2C%202023.pdf) of "do no harm".
# Asserting Data Sovereignty through Licensing and Procurement
The expansion of generative AI has introduced a structural imbalance into the humanitarian data model. Information is currently absorbed into training pipelines that generate enormous economic value for commercial entities, often without the participation or knowledge of the communities represented in the data. This dynamic has been described by scholars as a form of "[Digital Colonialism](https://www.ia-forum.org/Content/ViewInternal_Document.cfm?contenttype_id=1&ContentID=20237)," where the informational capital of the Global South is [harvested](https://www.ia-forum.org/Content/ViewInternal_Document.cfm?contenttype_id=1&ContentID=20237) to refine proprietary models owned by the [Global North](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/75/5/385/8078024).
### Digital Colonialism in the Global South
Unlike researchers or humanitarian practitioners, large AI developers rarely interact directly with the communities represented in their training data. As a result, the trust and accountability that govern the humanitarian social contract do not transfer into the [AI ecosystem](https://studenttheses.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A4287618/download). This extraction of data and digital labor turns human experience into raw material for [profit](https://unctad.org/news/ai-market-projected-hit-48-trillion-2033-emerging-dominant-frontier-technology) and [political influence](https://www.ictworks.org/ai-preparedness-index-shows-global-south-is-not-ready-for-artificial-intelligence-soltuions/). In some cases, tech companies outsource traumatizing content moderation tasks to workers in the Global South for minimal wages, further entrenching colonial patterns of control.

| **Digital Infrastructure**               | High data costs; 22% school electricity.   | Dependency on North-owned tools.      |

The IMF AI Preparedness Index reveals that most Global South countries lack the foundational infrastructure to make AI work sustainably, creating a dynamic of "digital apartheid" where countries become passive consumers of [Global North technology.](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/75/5/385/8078024) To address this, OCHA must lead the transition from human-readable disclaimers to Machine-Readable Ethics (MRE).
### The Implementation of AI-Specific Humanitarian Data Licenses
To mitigate the extractive nature of AI scraping, OCHA should explore the adoption of AI-specific humanitarian data licensing frameworks. Such frameworks would move beyond the limitations of standard robots.txt files and implement specialized metadata headers signaling "[Humanitarian Sensitivity](https://www.scribd.com/document/923128359/Ethical-Challenges-in-Open-Source-Software-Development-1)". These licenses would not represent a retreat from openness, but a [redefinition](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/do-no-harm-guide-global-perspectives.pdf) of it that protects humanitarian principles.

Prohibitions in such licensing frameworks could include:
- [Restrictions](https://permies.com/t/365913/a/280781/The-Regenerative-Governance-Model-CAL-License.pdf?download_attachment=true) on military or surveillance applications for humanitarian datasets.
- [Prohibitions](https://www.scribd.com/document/923128359/Ethical-Challenges-in-Open-Source-Software-Development-1) on the use of data for training commercial models without explicit, programmatic authorization.
- Mandatory programmatic [attribution](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/02adcae8-17f2-4ce0-9e70-9a925edf9542/download/guidance-note-on-data-security-in-operational-data-management.pdf) and [citation](https://wp.oecd.ai/app/uploads/2025/05/gpai-data-governance-work-framework-paper.pdf) of humanitarian data sources and their associated responsibility caveats.    
- [Reciprocity](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Facilitating-Data-Flows-through-Data-Collaboratives-A-Practical-Guide-to-Designing-Valuable-Accessible-and-Responsible-Data-Collaboratives.pdf) clauses requiring companies benefiting from humanitarian data to contribute resources or infrastructure [back](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/how-the-global-south-can-chart-a-new-path-for-ai/) to the sector.

![[Pasted image 20260313102746.png|Ethical licensing enables crisis-affected communities to benefit from the data they share|550]]

This approach is supported by the emergence of "[ethical licenses](https://oliviagallucci.com/literature-reviews-on-open-source/)" in the [open-source world,](https://www.scribd.com/document/923128359/Ethical-Challenges-in-Open-Source-Software-Development-1) such as the Hippocratic License, which restricts usage for purposes that violate human rights. While the legal enforceability of such licenses remains a challenge, their adoption establishes a standard for ethical procurement. OCHA must ultimately implement standards that disqualify technology partners whose parent companies provide algorithmic support for lethal autonomous systems or mass surveillance in conflict zones.
# Technical Architecture for Tiered Access and Privacy Preservation
Not all humanitarian datasets carry the same level of risk. While aggregated historical indicators may be shared in open formats, information relating to health status, protection incidents, or displacement movements requires significantly stronger [safeguards](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/8bc5b848-8ece-4f1f-a78b-18dd972bb21a/download/data-responsibility-guidelines-2025.pdf). Existing platforms that rely on bulk download models are increasingly vulnerable to automated crawlers that can replicate entire [repositories](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/repository-for-pdf-files) within hours.
### The Transition to Tiered Data Access Models
To mitigate this vulnerability, OCHA is transitioning toward tiered data access models where data availability is calibrated based on sensitivity and the potential for [misuse](https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/files2025/notizie/unep_cpr_asc_12_2_add-1-1.pdf).

| Access Tier          | Description of Data Category                   | Intended User Group               | Access Mechanism        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Open Access Tier** | Aggregated indicators, historical stats.       | General public, AI bots.          | Bulk download / API.    |
| **Registered Tier**  | Datasets requiring institutional verification. | Accredited partners, researchers. | Authentication portal.  |
| **Controlled Tier**  | Highly sensitive protection or health data.    | Specialist humanitarian teams.    | Secure APIs / Enclaves. |

Replacing bulk downloads with API-mediated access for sensitive indicators provides several critical advantages for data stewardship. Monitoring allows OCHA to observe who is accessing data and for what [purpose](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Facilitating-Data-Flows-through-Data-Collaboratives-A-Practical-Guide-to-Designing-Valuable-Accessible-and-Responsible-Data-Collaboratives.pdf). Rate-limiting prevents large-scale automated scraping by AI training [bots](https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/files2025/notizie/unep_cpr_asc_12_2_add-1-1.pdf). Auditability enables retrospective investigation of [suspicious](https://centre.humdata.org/hdx-year-in-review-2025/) usage patterns. This model mirrors the industry-wide move toward Privacy-Preserving Technologies (PPTs), where sensitive data is queried via secure interfaces rather than copied into unmonitored [local environments](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/02adcae8-17f2-4ce0-9e70-9a925edf9542/download/guidance-note-on-data-security-in-operational-data-management.pdf).

### Machine-Readable Ethics and Signal Protocols
The implementation of "no-train" zones for specific datasets requires a shift toward metadata-driven governance. By implementing specialized headers that signal sensitivity to AI crawlers, OCHA can programmatically enforce its [Data Responsibility Guidelines](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/8bc5b848-8ece-4f1f-a78b-18dd972bb21a/download/data-responsibility-guidelines-2025.pdf). This ensures that any AI model utilizing OCHA data must [cite](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11333263.pdf) the HDX caveats alongside its output, preventing the "context gap" that leads to operational mistakes.
# The Humanitarian AI Paradox and Human-in-the-loop
A critical challenge identified in 2025 is the "humanitarian AI paradox": while 93% of practitioners [report](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally) using AI tools, only 38% believe these tools improve decision-making. This contradiction suggests that while the adoption of technology is ubiquitous, confidence in its reliability remains low. This stems from the recognition that AI often captures quantitative indicators but misses the "lived realities" of [risk](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11333263.pdf).
### Expert Validation and the Workflow of Responsibility
To turn AI into a humanitarian ally, organizations must guide it through [expert verification](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally). The process involves:
1. **AI Analysis:** Rapid synthesis of displacement figures, food security trends, and protection incidents.
2. **Human Verification:** expert validation of AI findings to ensure accuracy and contextual relevance.
3. **Customization:** Adapting the outputs for specific audiences, such as inter-agency situation updates or donor reports.

Consider the 2025 GANNET Myanmar Situation Hub [case study](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/human-loop-how-oversight-turns-ai-humanitarian-ally): the AI system identified a correlates between displacement routes and checkpoints faster than human analysts. However, human verification was required to interpret what this meant for conditions in the field and the safety of aid delivery. Similarly, in Chad, AI flagged that distance to water sources created "protection concerns," but human analysts recognized that this meant heightened exposure to gender-based violence for girls walking over 5 kilometers.

| Phase of Humanitarian AI Workflow | AI Role                 | Human Analyst Role         | Risk if Human Absent         |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Data Collection**               | Multilingual ingestion. | Verification of sources.   | Ingestion of misinformation. |
| **Analysis**                      | Pattern identification. | Contextual interpretation. | Hallucinations/Mistakes.     |
| **Reporting**                     | Summarization.          | Auditory tailoring.        | Dehumanized/Biased tone.     |
| **Deployment**                    | Automated alerts.       | Ethical oversight.         | Dual-use for surveillance.   |

The objective is to guide AI rather than replace it, ensuring that innovation does not bypass the "Do No Harm" [principle](https://zenodo.org/records/10625799/files/2024-02-DoNoHarm-SOOCon24%20-%20Government,%20Law%20&%20Policy-2.pdf?download=1).
# Geopolitical Trends and the Proliferation of Dual-Use Technologies
As 2026 begins, the humanitarian sector faces a [breaking point](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point). Entrenched conflicts are more violent against civilians than at any time since World War II. The proliferation of [advanced technology](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/digitalage/artificial-intelligence-military-domain-briefer-1-en.pdf) in these zones, including AI and drones, has significant implications for how wars are waged and how aid is protected.
### Drone Proliferation and Data Sensitivity
Drone attacks in conflict settings [increased](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point) by 4,000% between 2020 and 2024, rising from 4,525 attacks in 2023 to nearly 20,000 in 2024. These technologies are also threatening life-saving aid delivery, with over 300 drone-related incidents affecting healthcare or aid delivery in 2024 alone. In this environment, the data OCHA collects—such as disaggregated population statistics and location markers for aid hubs—becomes extremely sensitive.

If this data is harvested by AI systems that lack humanitarian guardrails, it can be used to coordinate attacks with [high precision](https://perryworldhouse.upenn.edu/news-and-insight/designing-lawful-military-ai-technical-and-legal-reflections-on-decision-support-and-autonomous-weapon-systems/). Reports from the war in Gaza suggest that AI-enabled decision support systems (AI-DSS) have already contributed to strikes that increased civilian harm due to technical limitations and a lack of contextual [oversight](https://perryworldhouse.upenn.edu/news-and-insight/designing-lawful-military-ai-technical-and-legal-reflections-on-decision-support-and-autonomous-weapon-systems/). US Big Tech companies have played a central role in supplying digital infrastructure and analytics tools used for mass surveillance in these [contexts](https://www.techpolicy.press/ai-firms-can-limit-military-surveillance-of-americans-what-about-everyone-else/).

# Ethics of Information Sharing: The Humanitarian Social Contract
At its heart, humanitarian data governance is not simply a technical issue—it is a question of trust. Communities affected by crisis share information with humanitarian organizations because they believe that doing so will improve their assistance and [protection](https://publications.iom.int/system/files/pdf/IOM-IDDS-2021_1.pdf). That trust forms the foundation of the humanitarian "social contract": vulnerable populations provide [information](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998222/) about their lives, and humanitarian actors commit to using that information [responsibly](https://migrantprotection.iom.int/fr/resources/manuals-toolkits-and-guidance/operational-guidance-data-responsibility-humanitarian).
### The Erosion of Trust and the Chilling Effect
Artificial intelligence challenges this relationship in subtle but profound ways. When data collected for humanitarian purposes can be silently absorbed into opaque algorithmic systems—systems that may serve commercial, political, or even military interests—the integrity of the contract begins to [erode](https://thecontrapuntal.com/digital-colonialism-how-tech-giants-control-food-aid/). If affected populations perceive data collection as a precursor to algorithmic surveillance, they will respond with silence—leaving the humanitarian sector blind in the moments of greatest [need](https://www.ia-forum.org/Content/ViewInternal_Document.cfm?contenttype_id=1&ContentID=20237).

This erosion of trust creates a chilling effect on disclosure. Information flows will narrow. Assessments will become incomplete. Humanitarian operations will lose one of their most valuable assets: reliable knowledge of the populations they [serve](https://thecontrapuntal.com/digital-colonialism-how-tech-giants-control-food-aid/). For this reason, the evolution from "Open by Default" to "Responsible by Design" is not a retreat from openness—it is a [necessary](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Facilitating-Data-Flows-through-Data-Collaboratives-A-Practical-Guide-to-Designing-Valuable-Accessible-and-Responsible-Data-Collaboratives.pdf) adaptation to a radically changed technological environment.
# Conclusion: Safeguarding Agency and Dignity in a Digital World
OCHA has long been a pioneer in humanitarian data coordination. In the age of generative AI, it must now also become a guardian of ethical data stewardship, ensuring that innovation strengthens rather than undermines [humanitarian values](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11333263.pdf). The challenge is not merely to manage data more securely, but to build a digital ecosystem where the dignity, safety, and agency of vulnerable communities remain the central [design principles](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/8bc5b848-8ece-4f1f-a78b-18dd972bb21a/download/data-responsibility-guidelines-2025.pdf).

To achieve the transition to "Responsible by Design," the following actions are essential:
- **Lead the Transition to Machine-Readable Ethics (MRE):** Implement specialized metadata headers that signal "Humanitarian Sensitivity" to AI crawlers and require AI models to programmatically cite data caveats.
- **Assert Data Sovereignty through Licensing:** Explore AI-specific humanitarian data licenses that prohibit use for military or surveillance applications and training for commercial models without explicit authorization.
- **Implement Tiered Access for Sensitive Data:** calibration of data availability based on sensitivity, replacing bulk downloads with API-mediated access for highly sensitive indicators to enable monitoring and auditability.
- **Establish Ethical Procurement Standards:** Disqualify technology partners whose parent companies provide algorithmic support for lethal autonomous systems or mass surveillance in conflict zones.
- **Champion "Human-in-the-Loop" Verification:** Maintain humanitarian analysts at the center of AI workflows to translate quantitative indicators into the lived realities of risk and vulnerability.

By taking these steps, OCHA can help ensure that humanitarian information remains a shared public good rather than a silent subsidy for private technological development or a tool for [algorithmic violence](https://www.ia-forum.org/Content/ViewInternal_Document.cfm?contenttype_id=1&ContentID=20237). The future of the humanitarian sector depends on maintaining the trust of the populations it serves through responsible, ethical, and principled data stewardship in an automated world.
